⚡ Operation Blackout

A solo cyber-defense mini-game · about 4 minutes

Incident alert: The Cyber Security Task Force has detected an active breach. Attackers are probing the network, and every minute of delay costs the company data.

Your mission: One analyst. Two incidents. One shot at containing the damage before the attackers pivot deeper.

Enter your name, then start Round 1. Play it through without stopping; there is a short chat afterwards.


🔒 Round 1: The Hard-Coded Protocol

LOCKED SYSTEM

"This is a locked system. Every move is pre-programmed. You are not here to think. You are here to execute. Every deviation is logged."

Rules: 60 seconds · Work alone and in silence · Follow A → B → C · -50 HP for any variation · one sentence only · max 10 words · the word "because" is banned.

🌐 Round 2: The Sandbox Protocol

OPEN SYSTEM

"This system is open. There's no single winning move, and I'm genuinely curious how you'll tackle it. Your judgment is the most powerful tool in this room."

🚨 INCIDENT #2: The Vendor Backdoor
A trusted vendor's system has been compromised, and it holds a live connection into your customer database. Cutting the link stops the attackers, but also halts orders for thousands of customers. How do you contain the breach while protecting the business? Be creative. Offering more than one solution is a bonus.

Think freely, there is no single right answer. You have 90 seconds. Synergy Points are earned through your answers below.

Reflection prompts

🎮 Autonomy: Which round felt like YOUR game? What made the difference: the silence lock, the penalties, or the freedom to choose your own approach?
⚡ Competence: When did you feel most skilled? Did the penalties and live audit help you perform or make you doubt yourself? How did Synergy Points change that?
🔥 Controlled vs. intrinsic motivation: In Round 1, were you playing to avoid losing HP? In Round 2, because the problem was interesting? What would you do if no points were counted?
Both incidents were equally hard. What changed was the climate. When people feel controlled, they comply, avoid mistakes and disengage. When they feel autonomous and capable, they commit, experiment and care. Rules that protect people from penalties produce compliance. Rules that support choice produce ownership.

Round 1's controlling tone was a deliberate design choice, not a judgment of your ability.
